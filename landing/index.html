Tributary is a gateway you run yourself. Upstream, one pooled provider key — OpenAI and Gemini keys both work, along with any OpenAI-compatible endpoint; downstream, a personal key for every member. Each request is metered, quota-checked, and IP-fingerprinted before it leaves your building.

A tributary is a small stream that feeds a river

Your provider key is the river. Your team's usage is the streams. Tributary sits between the two: members draw small, governed streams — the pool stays whole. The owner holds the provider key (envelope-encrypted at rest) and decides who draws how much; odd signals surface as review flags, not silent surprises.

Per-member keys

Issue, rotate, expire, IP-allowlist. A member's key never touches the provider credential — and the raw key is shown exactly once, at issue time.

Leak signals, human responses

Key used from two networks? Off-purpose requests? Auto-block suspends the key and waits for a person — detection never silently bans anyone.

A full audit log

Every control-plane mutation — who, what, when, from where. Quota denials, rotations, approvals: all on the record.

Two minutes to first login

One container, one volume, SQLite inside — Docker does the rest. (POSIX shell; Windows users: WSL.) 

git clone https://github.com/Phat1911/Tributary.git && cd Tributary
cp deploy/.env.example .env
echo "TRIBUTARY_MASTER_KEY=$(openssl rand -hex 32)" >> .env
docker compose up -d
docker exec -it -e TRIBUTARY_DATABASE_URL=sqlite:///data/tributary.db \
  tributary tributary create-admin --email you@example.com --password '<generated>'

Then log in at http://localhost:8111. From there the ladder is yours to climb: this machine → your LAN → your tailnet → a public VPS — see the sharing ladder in the deploy guide.

Honest answers to the obvious questions

How is this different from OpenRouter or API relays?

They are hosted routers — one company's pipes. Your keys, traffic, and usage data pass through their building. Tributary is the building you own: self-hosted, source-available, your rules, your data. And on the market side they route spend — they don't settle surplus credits between strangers.

Why does reselling credits need a blockchain?

Between friends, trust is fine. Between strangers, someone has to hold the receipts — and if the operator holds them, the operator grades their own homework. The optional V2 layer makes escrow a program no single person can move, payouts un-rewritable, and every money moment a public record. Inside one org you never see it.

Why would anyone sell below the official price?

Nobody resells credits they bought at face value — and we say so publicly. The market lives on credits nobody bought: grants, hackathon prizes, education allocations, expiring promos, and committed spend a team has already paid for but won't consume. For that supply the cost basis is zero or sunk — selling at half of face is salvage, not a price war. Same economics as the gift-card resale industry. A pool of N keys also serves N× the rate limits — for AI agents, speed runs out before money.

Why Solana and not another chain?

The chain here is plumbing: micro-USD payouts, frequent windows. That needs fees that don't eat a micro-payment, finality in seconds, and mature tooling. Solana adds two things we'd have to fake elsewhere: Token-2022's native NonTransferable receipts (claims that cannot trade, by standard, not by promise) and a 2-of-2 Squads multisig vault. Users never need a wallet — so it's an engineering choice, not a religion, and multi-chain remains a roadmap option if operators ever pull elsewhere.

Where does the first dollar come from?

V1 is free forever, by strategy. The first dollar Tributary plans to earn is an operator's settlement fee (a fixed 5–10% band) on V2 marketplace volume — paid by operators, never by members. V2's launch gate includes provider/reseller agreements for credit transfer; until that gate opens, this is exactly what you see: a free, self-hosted governance tool. No dollar is planned from ToS-gray flows.

Does my data stay in my building?

Yes — with one stated exception. The optional purpose classifier is off by default, disclosed to members, and sends prompts only to the classifier provider you configure. The default flow keeps keys and usage inside your infrastructure, and the audit log is append-only at the database level — direct UPDATE or DELETE is refused by the engine itself.
